## Design Context

### Users
**Primary**: Students (high school / university) and knowledge workers (developers, writers, designers).
Students need strong, reliable friction against social media and YouTube during study sessions — motivated by streaks and visible progress. Knowledge workers need minimal setup friction and a system they can trust to hold them accountable during deep work sessions. Both groups respond to gamification but will abandon anything that feels patronizing or babyish.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Focused. Sharp. Serious.
The brand is not a gentle companion — it is a discipline-building system. Think coach, not cheerleader. The emotional goal is motivation through measurable progress: users should feel they are *earning* something, building a track record, and improving over time.

### Aesthetic Direction
**References**: Freedom (serious, reliable, no-nonsense productivity), Forest (gamified focus with visible progress and reward), Linear (sharp, fast, opinionated SaaS UI with exceptional typography and micro-detail).
**Anti-references**: Notion (too generic), generic "wellness" apps (too soft), corporate enterprise tools (too cold and bureaucratic).
**Visual tone**: Clean but with personality — not sterile. Type-forward. Progress indicators and discipline metrics should feel rewarding, not clinical. Dark and light both viable; current light-mode lavender palette is a reasonable foundation but needs more sharpness and confidence.
**Theme**: Light mode. Deep purple (#4a1d96) primary is correct — it reads as serious and intentional.

### Design Principles
1. **Friction is the product** — the interface should reinforce that resisting distraction is a virtue. Design should never undermine the user's intention to resist.
2. **Progress must be visible** — discipline is built through consistent behavior. Streaks, scores, and improvement signals should be present even in the free tier, not hidden behind Pro.
3. **Sharp over soft** — prefer clarity, weight, and precision over rounded softness. Typography and spacing should carry authority.
4. **Earn every word** — copy should be direct and purposeful. No filler, no over-explanation. The brand speaks like a focused coach.
5. **Consistency at all surfaces** — the popup, options page, friction page, and welcome page should feel like one product, not four separate utilities.
